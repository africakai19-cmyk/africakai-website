SARS Tax Clearance: Tax Compliance Status (TCS)

What tax clearance means today, when you need it, and why applications get blocked.

By AfricaKai · Last reviewed 13 October 2026

If you have ever been asked for a “tax clearance certificate” for a tender, a contract or a funder, what you actually need today is a Tax Compliance Status (TCS) PIN from SARS. This guide explains what it is and how to get one.

What is Tax Compliance Status (TCS)?

Tax Compliance Status is the SARS system that shows whether your tax affairs are in order. It replaced the old tax clearance certificate. Instead of a paper certificate, SARS issues a TCS PIN that you can give to a third party so they can verify your status online.

Is it the same as a tax clearance certificate?

Effectively, yes. Many people still say “tax clearance” or “tax clearance certificate”, and tender documents or clients sometimes still use those words. SARS replaced the paper certificate with the Tax Compliance Status system, so what you actually provide today is your TCS PIN. If someone asks you for tax clearance, they almost always mean this.

When do you need it?

SARS says businesses are typically asked to prove their compliance when applying for a tender, entering a new contract, confirming good standing, or in respect of foreign investment. Clients, funders and procurement systems often ask for it too.

How is it issued?

The PIN is requested through your SARS eFiling profile, and SARS issues it if your tax affairs are in order. Each request produces a new PIN, and the person verifying your status needs your tax reference number and that PIN. BizPortal does not offer TCS, so it has to go through SARS.

Your status is not fixed

Your compliance status changes with your ongoing compliance. If you were compliant when you applied but miss a return or payment later, the status a third party sees will reflect where you are today.

Why applications get blocked

In practice, the PIN itself is rarely the problem. What blocks it is the compliance behind it:

  • Outstanding tax returns, sometimes going back several years.
  • Unpaid balances or penalties on your SARS account.
  • Tax types you should be registered for but are not.
  • Details on file with SARS that are out of date or do not match your company records.

SARS shows the status against each compliance requirement, so you can see where the problem is. If you believe the status is wrong, you can challenge it with SARS.

Why timing matters

Clearing a backlog takes time, and tender and contract deadlines do not wait. If you know a tender, funding application or new client is coming, check your compliance status well in advance rather than the week the document is due.
