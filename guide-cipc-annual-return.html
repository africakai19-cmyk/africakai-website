CIPC Annual Return: What You Need to Know

Deadlines, penalties and deregistration risk, explained in plain English for South African business owners.

By AfricaKai · Last reviewed 12 October 2026

If you own a registered company in South Africa, there is one filing that quietly decides whether your company stays on the register: the CIPC annual return. This guide explains what it is, when it is due and what can happen if you miss it.

What is a CIPC annual return?

An annual return is a yearly filing with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) that confirms your company is still active and that its details are correct. It is not a tax return. It goes to CIPC, not SARS.

Who has to file one?

Every registered company must file an annual return every year. This applies even if the company did not trade or earn any income during the year.

When is it due?

Your annual return is tied to your company’s registration anniversary, the date it was originally registered with CIPC. You must file within 30 business days after that anniversary. For example, if your company was registered on 15 August, your return falls due each year after 15 August. Weekends and public holidays do not count as business days, but 30 business days still passes faster than most owners expect.

You can find your registration date on your company registration certificate.

How is it filed, and what does it cost?

Annual returns are filed electronically through CIPC’s online services. The fee is set by CIPC and is higher if you file late, so always check CIPC’s current fee table before paying. If you want to avoid the admin, we can file it for you.

Annual return vs SARS tax return

Many owners confuse the two. The CIPC annual return keeps your company registered. Your income tax return is a separate obligation with SARS. Filing one does not satisfy the other, so a compliant company needs both.

What happens if you do not file?

  • Penalties: CIPC charges more for returns filed outside the 30-business-day window.
  • Deregistration: CIPC can deregister companies that fail to file, typically after sustained non-compliance.
  • Business problems: a company that is not in good standing can struggle to apply for funding, open or keep business bank accounts, or tender for work.

If your company has already been deregistered, there is a formal process to have it reinstated. It is far easier and cheaper to stay compliant than to fix it later.

Other CIPC changes to keep up to date

CIPC must also be notified when your company’s directors change, within 10 business days of the change. Beneficial ownership information must be filed and kept current as well. We handle both as part of ongoing CIPC filings.

A simple checklist

  • Find your registration anniversary date on your certificate and put a reminder in your calendar.
  • Confirm your company details (directors, address, ownership) are up to date with CIPC.
  • File within 30 business days of the anniversary, even if the company did not trade.
  • Keep your SARS income tax return and other filings separate and up to date.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. CIPC rules and fees can change, so confirm current requirements with CIPC or speak to us.
