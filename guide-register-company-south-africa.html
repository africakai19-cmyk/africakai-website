How to Register a Company in South Africa

Everything you need to have ready, and what to do once your company is registered.

By AfricaKai · Last reviewed 13 October 2026

Registering a company is the first step to running a credible, properly structured business. Here is what you need to have ready, how the process works and what to do straight after your company is registered.

Which type of company should I register?

For most small businesses and startups, the right choice is a private company, known as a (Pty) Ltd. It is a separate legal entity from its owners. Sole proprietors who trade under their own name do not have to register with CIPC, but a company gives you more credibility and access to funding, partnerships and larger clients.

What you need before you start

  • At least one director. A company needs a minimum of one director.
  • Identity details for each director. This means a valid South African ID number for South African directors, or a passport and supporting documents for non-South African directors. Certified copies may be requested.
  • Contact details. Full names, email addresses and cellphone numbers for every director.
  • Addresses. Residential and postal addresses, and a physical address in South Africa for the company.
  • A company name. You can reserve a name with CIPC, or register with a number-based name and choose a name later. Check the name is available before you commit to branding.
  • A Memorandum of Incorporation (MOI). Every company must have one. It is the rulebook for how the company operates. Many small businesses use the standard MOI, while some need a customised one.

How the registration process works

  1. Decide the company type, directors and name.
  2. Gather the director details and documents listed above.
  3. Submit the registration to CIPC, typically online, together with the MOI.
  4. Complete the identity verification and signing steps.
  5. Receive your registration documents from CIPC and check every detail: company name, registration number, directors and addresses.

Common reasons registrations get delayed

  • The proposed name is rejected or is too close to an existing company.
  • Director details do not match their ID exactly, or documents are incomplete.
  • Unsigned MOI pages or mismatched addresses across documents.

What to do after your company is registered

  • Register for tax with SARS. Every company must be registered as a taxpayer. Depending on your business this may also include VAT and PAYE.
  • Open a business bank account. Banks usually ask for your registration documents, director IDs and proof of address.
  • Register as an employer. If you will hire staff, you will need UIF and COIDA registration as well.
  • Diarise your annual return. It falls due each year after your registration anniversary. See our CIPC annual return guide.
  • Keep CIPC up to date. Notify CIPC when directors change, and keep beneficial ownership information current.

You can do all of this yourself. Many owners prefer to have it handled for them so the details are right the first time and nothing gets missed afterwards.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Requirements, forms and fees can change, so confirm current details with CIPC or speak to us.
